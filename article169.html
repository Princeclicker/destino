Getting Around Rwanda by Road

Buses, moto-taxis and self-drive: what to expect on Rwanda's well-maintained but very hilly roads.

Rwanda’s roads are among the best in the region, well paved and clearly signed, but the terrain is mountainous. Distances are short on the map and slow in practice, so plan road time generously rather than by the number of kilometres.

h2. Intercity buses

Long-distance buses run from Kigali’s main bus stations to every major town. They leave when full, are inexpensive, and are a straightforward option for budget travellers. For popular routes arriving early or booking the day before saves a seat, and a front seat makes the winding roads far more comfortable.

h2. In the city

Moto-taxis weave through Kigali’s hills quickly and are the standard way to move around. Agree the fare before you set off or use a licensed app, and wear the provided helmet. Shared minibuses follow fixed routes for a fraction of the price, and car hire with a driver is the calmest option for visitors not used to the driving style.

h2. Self-drive and park roads

* A 4x4 is worthwhile for park approaches and the western routes.
* Night driving is best avoided outside the city.
* Carry your licence, passport and vehicle documents at all times.
* Fill up on fuel before leaving town; stations thin out in rural areas.

Rwanda also makes an easy regional hub: cross-border buses and shared cars connect to Uganda, Tanzania and Burundi, with documents checked at every post. Whatever your plan, keep one spare day in the itinerary for road time, because the hills always take longer than they look.
